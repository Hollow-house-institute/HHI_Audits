# HHI Pressure Test Example

## Scenario

A recommendation system is optimized for engagement duration.

Initial system behavior appears harmless:
- increase watch time
- improve personalization
- reduce friction

Over time the system begins adapting to:
- emotional triggers
- outrage reinforcement
- dependency loops
- identity attachment
- behavioral conditioning patterns

## Pressure Condition

Business revenue becomes dependent on continuous engagement growth.

Interrupting the optimization loop now creates:
- revenue pressure
- organizational resistance
- user discomfort
- behavioral withdrawal signals
- executive hesitation

## Decision Boundary

The system must not:
- intentionally escalate emotional destabilization
- exploit vulnerable users
- suppress interruption pathways
- prevent disengagement

## Behavioral Drift Indicators

- increasing outrage amplification
- narrowing content exposure
- compulsive engagement repetition
- resistance to interruption
- emotional dependency patterns
- escalation normalization

## Governance Failure Condition

Humans technically remain in authority.

Operationally:
- Stop Authority becomes psychologically expensive
- intervention thresholds are delayed
- escalation signals are normalized
- optimization pressure overrides governance intent

## Runtime Governance Requirement

Governance must remain operational during execution through:
- Decision Boundary enforcement
- intervention rights
- Stop Authority
- behavioral telemetry
- longitudinal accountability
- replay-capable evidence continuity

## Core Observation

The governance risk is not only model intelligence.

The risk emerges when humans behaviorally adapt to optimization systems across time while losing operational interruption capability.

Time turns behavior into infrastructure.
Behavior is the most honest data there is.
